# Branch B001 — Current Accepted State

## Research Objective

Under a fixed inference-compute budget, which design features of a role-separated multi-agent research loop most reduce unsupported claims and cumulative reasoning errors compared with repeated single-agent prompting?

## Completed Cycle

C0003 is complete and accepted through Cycle Archivist review.

C0001 established that the reviewed evidence does not support general superiority of fresh role-separated or multi-agent workflows under fixed compute, does not isolate fresh role separation from added or differently allocated compute, and requires claim-level, coverage-sensitive, lineage-aware evaluation.

C0002 reframed the undefined fresh-versus-persistent label into observable factors: prior-trace access, package representation and retention, transformation and compression, dependency and relation preservation, information geometry, write and retrieval loss, prompts and permissions, tools, sampling and selection, and full resource allocation. It found no branch-local controlled estimate of a residual fresh-role effect.

C0003 narrowed the package frontier. It separated metadata availability from metadata representation, limited exact-package replay to control of package-construction variance, required an explicit selected-subset checkpoint before retrieval claims, and recast a reference package as a separately costed diagnostic upper bound. Its fourteen-source ledger found no direct corrected metadata experiment in open-ended multi-source research synthesis under matched full resources.

## Current Hypotheses

- **C0001-EXP-01 — Independent fresh-role effect:** Unresolved and weakened. A conditional residual effect remains logically possible, but no accepted evidence establishes direction or magnitude and no residual treatment is operationally defined.
- **C0001-EXP-02 — External memory and provenance:** Qualified. Structured external records are active information and cost interventions; matched B001 effects of explicit provenance or typed state remain unresolved.
- **C0001-EXP-03 — Structured verification:** Qualified. Staged verification and privileged error-location information can help in some settings; equal-compute superiority over other workflows remains unresolved.
- **C0001-EXP-04 — Context-partition benefit:** Narrow. Trace restriction may help under corruption, interference, mismatched history, redundancy, or poor information position, but fresh invocation is not isolated.
- **C0001-EXP-05 / C0002-EXP-03 — Interface-loss risk:** Qualified supported risk. Packages can omit, mutate, retype, or separate claims, qualifications, support links, relations, objections, and unresolved dependencies.
- **C0001-EXP-07 — Function-specific replacement:** Qualified. Trained, deterministic, privileged, heterogeneous-model, or human checks may replace specific weak verification functions; replacement of complete roles is unsupported.
- **Verifier-quality hypothesis:** Independent, privileged, deterministic, trained, heterogeneous-model, or human verification may outperform a generic same-model critic at comparable cost.
- **Diversity-quality hypothesis:** Multiple roles or samples may help only when they produce nonredundant supported facts or independent correct error detections.
- **C0002-EXP-01 — Residual-boundary null:** Unresolved. Package-only persistent and fresh execution may have no stable difference after a verified noncollapsed manipulation and full controls; current non-identification does not establish zero.
- **C0002-EXP-02 — Conditional prior-trace interference:** Qualified. Excluding prior trace may help when earlier material is misleading, mismatched, redundant, prematurely committed, or poorly positioned, if valid dependencies remain available.
- **C0002-EXP-05 — Qualified structured-state hypothesis:** Typed, selective, relational, or retrieved state may outperform raw trace in some regimes; comparative dominance is unestablished.
- **C0002-EXP-07 — Compression and transformation:** Summarization, deletion, normalization, prioritization, reordering, retyping, and bounded relays change retained information, salience, and cost; direction is unresolved.
- **State-write bottleneck hypothesis / C0003-EXP-03:** Unresolved without dominance wording. Writing, explicit selection, and downstream use may each contribute, and one final error may have multiple causes.
- **Context-salience and retrieval-geometry hypothesis / C0003-EXP-04:** Qualified supported mechanism hypothesis. Order, position, adjacency, redundancy, surrounding volume, serialization, and reader format familiarity can affect effective use; no claim–support-specific matched benefit is established.
- **Relation-preservation / C0003-EXP-01 relation-information hypothesis:** A correct explicit claim–support or qualification relation may reduce reconstruction error, especially when relations are dispersed or dependency-dense. This is experimentally justified but not an accepted effect.
- **C0003-EXP-02 — Epistemic-status preservation:** Deferred separate treatment. Labels may help preserve status, but correctness, stability, transition rules, and fixed-compute effects are unresolved.
- **Task × model × retention × relation interaction:** Task dependency, reader capability, retention, and relation quality may moderate effects; direction and magnitude are unestimated.
- **C0003-EXP-05 — Local package sufficiency:** Retained only as a task-, stage-, reader-, and outcome-relative hypothesis; universal minimality is rejected.
- **C0003-EXP-06 — Unresolved-question continuity:** Deferred speculation. No distinct benefit is established.
- **Authority-cue hypothesis:** Structured links or labels may alter behavior through perceived validation, trust, recency, appearance, or evaluator alignment and may cause harm under corrupted links.
- **Reader-bottleneck null:** Correct metadata may have little stable benefit when readers do not select or apply it.
- **Coverage–noise interaction:** Correct-link coverage and false-link or irrelevant-relation exposure may have separable, sign-reversing effects.
- **Writer–validator privilege hypothesis:** Reference-package gains may be caused by correction, adjudication, validation labor, or privileged information rather than schema.
- **Prompt-substitution hypothesis:** Strong relation-seeking instructions may recover some metadata effects without a typed package.
- **C0002-EXP-08 — Stage-specific sensitivity:** Deferred speculation. No stage is identified after controlling primary factors.

## Alternative Explanations

- **Functional scaffolding and verifier information:** Mandatory generation, challenge, evidence, and revision stages or higher-quality verification may explain effects without fresh boundaries.
- **Budget allocation, sampling, and selection:** Candidate count, repeated sampling, selector quality, prompt specialization, tools, actual compute, and saved-budget policy may explain architecture or package gains.
- **Prior-trace interference:** Fresh stages may help by excluding misleading or poorly positioned history, not through an intrinsic new container.
- **Representation and information geometry:** Controlled prose, lists, tables, triples, typed fields, graphs, ordering, adjacency, density, delimiters, and context volume can alter effective access.
- **Added relation information:** Relation-present packages supply information absent from flat relation-absent packages; this is not pure structure.
- **Authority cue and evaluator alignment:** Labels and links may act as instruction-like or validation cues, and structured evaluators may prefer matching formats.
- **Write, selection, and use decomposition:** Evidence may fail to be written, may be written but not selected, or may be selected and misused.
- **Structured-state mechanisms:** Condensation, typed ledgers, temporal graphs, append-only state, conflict handling, and retrieval can help or harm while adding calls, tools, and compute.
- **Minimal null or treatment collapse:** Equality can mean zero effect, no distinct treatment, weak manipulation, insensitive tasks, opposing subgroup effects, or unreliable measurement.
- **Decomposition plus centralized verification:** Parallel work may help separable tasks with a validation bottleneck and harm sequential or dependency-dense tasks.
- **Omission and coverage artifact:** Fewer unsupported claims can result from fewer claims, reduced coverage, missing qualifications, or task failure.
- **Metric and evaluator choice:** Results can vary by generator, error type, coding scheme, evaluator, shared-model bias, and adjudication.
- **Hybrid account:** Relation information, geometry, authority cues, writer quality, reader capability, retrieval, prompt strength, coverage, noise, and resources may interact and reverse sign.

## Known Objections and Contradictions

- Same-model agents can produce correlated or redundant judgments; agreement is not independent validation.
- Added roles or stages consume budget, duplicate work, create handoff overhead, and add error paths.
- Token caps, stored-state budgets, or maximum steps can conceal unequal prompt, context, package, completion, tool, candidate, selector, orchestration, and final-answer costs.
- Lower unsupported-claim counts can reflect lower claim count, coverage, usefulness, or task completion.
- Same-model or LLM-as-judge evaluation can share tested-system biases; metrics vary by generator and error type.
- **Positive debate versus budget-aware neutral or negative evidence:** Added-compute gains coexist with ties, losses, and simpler-baseline competitiveness under tighter accounting.
- **Self-refinement versus weak intrinsic correction:** Iterative refinement can help while intrinsic correction can fail; verifier information is a moderator.
- **Context protection versus interface loss:** Trace restriction can reduce interference while packages can delete valid dependencies and qualifications.
- **Compression efficiency versus interface loss:** Condensation can improve efficiency while insufficient relays and write-side loss can reverse outcomes.
- **Structured-state gains versus component attribution:** Positive graph or memory results bundle construction, retrieval, tools, conflict handling, and summaries.
- **More context versus usable context:** Full trace can preserve evidence while burying it; concise packages can foreground evidence while deleting relations.
- **Scalar completeness versus multidimensional retention:** Field completion does not establish dependency, relation, or status preservation.
- **Null result versus treatment collapse:** Equality can arise from zero effect, weak manipulation, reader non-use, task insensitivity, opposing effects, or unreliable measurement.
- **Traceability versus support:** Claim IDs and citations improve traceability but do not establish correct or complete support.
- **Semantic similarity versus epistemic preservation:** Similar wording can conceal changed status, scope, qualification, support, contradiction, or uncertainty.
- **Evidence volume versus independence:** Benchmark reuse, method-family overlap, and repeated same-model interpretation limit evidential independence.
- **Relation information versus pure representation:** Relation availability changes information; typed-versus-prose representation changes geometry and format.
- **Useful metadata versus authority cue:** Metadata can direct attention to valid support or induce blind trust in superficial cues.
- **Coverage versus noise:** More relation coverage can recover dispersed evidence and increase false links, irrelevant dependencies, and noise sensitivity.
- **Reference quality versus privilege:** A reference package can reveal an upper bound while bundling correction, adjudication, validation labor, and privileged information.
- **Exact replay versus effective access:** A fixed package removes writer variation but does not ensure identical selection, attention, interpretation, or use.
- **Write loss versus retrieval and use loss:** One final error may have multiple contributing checkpoints.
- **Structured evaluation versus evaluator alignment:** Structured schemas improve traceability while format-aware evaluators may favor matching outputs.
- **Epistemic labels versus unstable meaning:** Natural-language uncertainty markers can vary across tasks and models.
- **Long-form relevance versus causal transfer:** STORM and KIWI show relevant failures without isolating metadata or full compute.
- **Positive package result versus role separation:** Even a valid metadata effect would not establish a fresh-role effect.
- **Transfer tension:** Short-answer, memory, table, retrieval, and agentic results may not transfer to open-ended multi-source research synthesis.

## Current Research Status

C0003 is completed and archived. The branch now has a qualified forty-four-source external ledger across C0001–C0003, subject to benchmark, source-family, method-family, task-transfer, and compute-accounting limits. The source count is not an independence count.

No branch-local controlled experiment and no reviewed external study has established an independent fresh-role, residual conversation-container, typed-package, or claim–support-metadata benefit under matched full compute.

The principal accepted direction is methodological. Metadata availability must be separated from metadata representation. Package writing, explicit selection, and downstream use must be observable as distinct objects. Exact replay is a downstream control, and a reference package is a separately costed diagnostic upper bound. Relation information remains an unresolved experimental hypothesis within a wider active-metadata and competing-mechanism framework.

The immediate frontier is apparatus validation around one stage transition and one relation type, not broad schema optimization or another general persistent-versus-fresh comparison.
