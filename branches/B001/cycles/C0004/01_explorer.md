# Explorer — C0004

## Selected Frontier

Validate whether explicit **claim–support relations** improve transfer across the **External Evidence Researcher → Alternative Theorist** handoff, while separating relation availability from representation and observing writing, selection, and downstream use as distinct checkpoints.

## Why This Frontier Now

The accepted branch state identifies apparatus validation around one stage transition and one relation type as the immediate frontier. Claim–support relations are narrower than a full typed package, directly connected to the branch objective of reducing unsupported claims, and compatible with the accepted requirement to distinguish relation availability from equivalent-content representation. The External Evidence Researcher → Alternative Theorist handoff is useful because it transfers source-linked claims into a stage that must generate competing explanations without converting plausible reinterpretation into unsupported fact.

This cycle should therefore explore the smallest useful design space for determining whether claim–support links are actually written, selected, and used—not whether typed packages, fresh roles, or the full seven-role loop are generally superior.

## Relevant Accepted Branch State

- **Accepted branch fact:** No branch-local controlled experiment or reviewed external study has established an independent fresh-role, residual conversation-container, typed-package, or claim–support-metadata benefit under matched full compute.
- **Accepted methodological correction:** Relation absent versus correctly supplied tests metadata availability; controlled prose versus a typed field carrying approximately equivalent relation content tests representation.
- **Accepted methodological requirement:** A valid package experiment must record a predeclared reference inventory, the written package, an explicit selected subset wherever retrieval or selection is claimed, and final claims with item-level lineage.
- **Accepted qualified claim:** Metadata, serialization, ordering, geometry, authority cues, reader capability, prompts, sampling, selection, coverage, noise, and resources remain live competing explanations for apparent package effects.
- **Accepted evaluation requirement:** Unsupported-claim outcomes must be interpreted with claim count, support correctness, factual coverage, citation correctness and completeness, task completion, usefulness, and omission severity.
- **Unresolved branch question:** It remains unknown whether correct explicit claim–support relations improve downstream reasoning after equivalent content, geometry, reader instructions, candidate opportunities, selection, evaluation, and end-to-end resources are controlled.

## Candidate Hypotheses and Possibilities

### C0004-EXP-01 — Relation-availability benefit

**Hypothesis:** Supplying correct explicit claim–support relations at the handoff reduces downstream support reconstruction errors, especially when several evidence items could plausibly support the same claim or when support is dispersed across the package.

**Distinguishing information:** Compare relation-absent packages with correct-relation packages while holding the underlying claims, evidence excerpts, source identifiers, order, surrounding context, prompts, and resource allocation as constant as possible.

### C0004-EXP-02 — Representation-equivalence hypothesis

**Hypothesis:** Most or all benefit attributed to typed claim–support edges can be reproduced by controlled prose that states the same relation content.

**Distinguishing information:** Compare controlled prose and typed fields with approximately equivalent relation content, matched adjacency, ordering, token distance, delimiters, and reader instructions.

### C0004-EXP-03 — Geometry-mediated use

**Hypothesis:** Apparent relation benefits arise mainly because explicit links move claims and evidence closer together, increase salience, or reduce search burden rather than because the reader interprets a distinct epistemic relation.

**Distinguishing information:** Vary representation while separately matching or manipulating adjacency, position, token distance, ordering, redundancy, and surrounding volume.

### C0004-EXP-04 — Authority-cue compliance

**Hypothesis:** A relation marker functions as a validation or authority cue, causing the downstream reader to trust linked material more readily whether the link is correct or not.

**Distinguishing information:** Include correct, corrupted, inert-placebo, neutrally framed, and explicitly validated relation conditions. Measure whether corrupted links are followed, rejected, or propagated.

### C0004-EXP-05 — Reader-selection bottleneck

**Hypothesis:** Correct relations have little effect unless the Alternative Theorist explicitly selects and uses the linked evidence; package presence alone is insufficient.

**Distinguishing information:** Require a selected-subset object before downstream generation and compare relation presence, relation selection, and final use at item level.

### C0004-EXP-06 — Coverage–noise interaction

**Hypothesis:** Increasing correct relation coverage initially improves support preservation but eventually creates clutter or distracts from the most decision-relevant evidence; false or irrelevant links may reverse the effect.

**Distinguishing information:** Predeclare relation-coverage levels and false-link or irrelevant-link exposure separately, while measuring both support recall and noise sensitivity.

### C0004-EXP-07 — Prompt-substitution possibility

**Hypothesis:** A strong relation-seeking instruction may recover much of the effect of explicit links by directing the reader to reconstruct support relations from unchanged content.

**Distinguishing information:** Compare neutral reader instructions with relation-seeking instructions under relation-absent and relation-present conditions, without changing candidate or selector opportunities.

### C0004-EXP-08 — Stage-local sensitivity

**Speculation:** The External Evidence Researcher → Alternative Theorist handoff may be unusually sensitive to claim–support relations because the receiving role is expected to transform evidence into competing explanations. This is only a cycle-local possibility; the accepted branch state does not establish that this transition is more sensitive than another transition.

**Distinguishing information:** First validate the apparatus locally. A later predeclared comparison with one other transition would be needed before making a stage-sensitivity claim.

## Possible Mechanisms or Connections

1. **Reconstruction reduction:** Explicit relations may reduce the need to infer which evidence supports which claim.
2. **Dependency preservation:** Links may preserve support structure when claims and evidence are separated during handoff.
3. **Attention and geometry:** Typed or prose links may foreground evidence through adjacency, compactness, ordering, or delimiters.
4. **Authority signaling:** Relation labels may be interpreted as prior validation rather than as neutral metadata.
5. **Selection mediation:** Relations may matter only by changing which items the downstream role selects for use.
6. **Error propagation:** Corrupted links may convert a local package error into a downstream supported-looking but invalid interpretation.
7. **Coverage–noise tradeoff:** More links may recover omitted support while increasing irrelevant dependencies and reader load.
8. **Prompt interaction:** Reader instructions may substitute for, amplify, or suppress explicit relation effects.

These mechanisms are not established effects. Several may operate together, and the first pilot should be designed to distinguish observable checkpoints rather than assign a single dominant cause prematurely.

## Questions for Internal Evidence Research

1. What exact claim, evidence, source, and support-link objects already appear in accepted branch conventions and prior authorized outputs?
2. Which branch-local transition records can define the External Evidence Researcher output and Alternative Theorist input without inventing a new full package schema?
3. What minimum fields are required to represent one claim–support relation while preserving claim identity, source identity, scope, and qualification?
4. Which existing branch rules can define a selected-subset object and final-use lineage without treating package presence as retrieval?
5. What branch-local examples illustrate claim split, merge, paraphrase, scope change, qualification weakening, or support substitution across stages?
6. Which accepted evaluation gates can be operationalized immediately for a pilot, and which remain undefined?
7. What exact resource-accounting fields are already required by the branch, and which must be specified before any comparison is interpretable?
8. Does the branch contain a defensible reason to prefer claim–support over claim–qualification for the first apparatus pilot, or is this only a pragmatic selection?

## Questions for External Evidence Research

1. Are there primary studies that directly compare explicit claim–support links with the same claim and evidence content presented without links?
2. Are there primary studies comparing equivalent relation content in controlled prose versus typed edges, fields, tables, graphs, or other structured forms?
3. What methods have been used to distinguish metadata use from adjacency, order, token distance, salience, delimiters, or format familiarity?
4. Are there validated paradigms using correct, corrupted, misleading, inert-placebo, or authority-framed links to separate reasoning benefit from cue compliance?
5. What primary work explicitly records retrieval or selected evidence before generation, allowing selection to be separated from downstream use?
6. What annotation schemes and reliability methods exist for claim identity, evidence linkage, split/merge events, scope changes, and multi-cause error lineage?
7. What evidence exists on relation-coverage versus false-link or irrelevant-link exposure, especially where both support recall and noise sensitivity are measured?
8. Are there fixed-resource or fully accounted studies in which relation metadata, reader prompts, candidates, selectors, and final integration are controlled together?
9. Are there open-ended multi-source synthesis studies using relation-aware handoffs between evidence collection and alternative-explanation generation?
10. What findings would justify transferring results from claim verification, retrieval, structured input, or long-context tasks to this specific staged synthesis handoff, and what limits block that transfer?

## Potential Tests or Comparisons

- **Availability contrast:** Same claims and evidence, with claim–support relations absent versus correctly supplied.
- **Representation contrast:** Approximately equivalent relation content in controlled prose versus typed claim–support fields.
- **Cue controls:** Correct relation, corrupted relation, inert-placebo marker, neutral framing, and validated framing.
- **Geometry controls:** Match or factorially vary adjacency, order, token distance, delimiters, redundancy, and surrounding context.
- **Selection checkpoint:** Require the receiving role to emit a machine-readable selected-subset object before producing alternatives.
- **Lineage checkpoint:** Trace each reference claim and evidence item through package writing, selection, final use, omission, mutation, contradiction, or unsupported extension.
- **Coverage–noise comparison:** Vary correct-link coverage independently from false-link and irrelevant-link rates.
- **Prompt interaction:** Neutral versus relation-seeking reader instructions under both relation-absent and relation-present conditions.
- **Reliability pilot:** Double-code a small predeclared sample for relation correctness, claim identity, and lineage events before running an effectiveness comparison.
- **Resource ledger:** Record writer, validator, prompt, context, completion, selection, candidate, and final-generation costs, with a declared saved-budget rule.

The initial use of these tests should be manipulation and measurement validation. They should establish that conditions differ as intended and that writing, selection, and use can be measured reliably before estimating a package-effect magnitude.

## Main Uncertainties

- Whether claim–support is the most informative first relation type rather than merely the most direct fit to the branch objective.
- Whether the selected stage transition is representative or unusually easy or difficult.
- Whether controlled prose and typed fields can be made sufficiently equivalent in meaning and geometry.
- Whether a selected-subset requirement changes the receiving role’s behavior enough to become part of the treatment.
- Whether relation correctness and claim identity can be annotated reliably across paraphrase, split, merge, and synthesis.
- Whether corrupted and inert controls isolate authority cues without introducing new wording or salience differences.
- Whether a pilot can satisfy coverage and usefulness gates under a fixed end-to-end budget.
- Whether any observed effect transfers beyond this stage, task set, reader model, package size, or relation-density regime.
- Whether a positive relation effect would survive prompt-equivalence, evaluator blinding, equal candidates and selectors, and full resource accounting.

## Handoff to Critic

Assess whether the External Evidence Researcher → Alternative Theorist transition and claim–support relation form a sufficiently narrow, noncollapsed pilot. Focus on whether the proposed contrasts genuinely separate relation availability, representation, geometry, authority cues, selection, and use; whether the selected-subset and lineage objects are measurable without becoming unequal interventions; and whether the proposed evaluation and resource gates are strong enough to prevent lower coverage or added compute from masquerading as reduced unsupported claims.
