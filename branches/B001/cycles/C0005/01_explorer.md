# Explorer — C0005

## Selected Frontier

Determine whether the External Evidence Researcher → Alternative Theorist handoff contains a sufficiently large and stable set of narrowly defined direct claim–support relations whose identities and downstream semantic use can be measured reliably before any relation-availability or representation-effect experiment is attempted.

This frontier is deliberately limited to **measurability and apparatus fit**. It does not test whether explicit relations improve performance, whether typed representation is superior to prose, whether the selected handoff is uniquely sensitive, or whether role separation is beneficial.

## Why This Frontier Now

The accepted branch state makes apparatus validation the immediate direction and identifies the selected handoff as only a pragmatic candidate. The most basic unresolved dependency is therefore not effect size but whether the proposed objects exist in a form that can be coded consistently and followed across the handoff.

A useful next step is to answer four linked questions:

1. Are there enough candidate direct-support pairs in realistic External Evidence Researcher outputs?
2. Can independent annotators distinguish direct support from contextualization, qualification, contradiction, exemplification, motivation, or ambiguous relevance?
3. Can claim and evidence identities survive paraphrase, split, merge, scope change, and qualification change?
4. Can downstream Alternative Theorist text be coded for actual semantic use rather than mere citation, copied identifiers, selection, or post hoc rationalization?

If these conditions are not met, a later relation treatment would be difficult to interpret. If they are met, the branch would have a concrete basis for specifying the minimum availability manipulation without claiming an effect in advance.

## Relevant Accepted Branch State

- **Accepted methodological direction:** `C0004-INTG-10` requires a fixed inventory and narrow direct-support codebook, a measurability screen at the selected handoff, observable write–select–use lineage, role-aligned scoring, coverage and usefulness gates, evaluator reliability, and full resource accounting before representation comparisons.
- **Accepted methodological narrowing:** `C0004-INTG-02` states that the next branch exercise may validate objects, manipulations, and measurements but cannot yet support a reliable claim–support performance-effect estimate.
- **Accepted codebook requirement:** `C0004-INTG-05` states that direct support must not be silently pooled with partial support, contextualization, exemplification, qualification, contradiction, motivation, or ambiguous many-to-many relevance.
- **Accepted lineage requirement:** `C0003-INTG-04` requires separate records for the reference inventory, written package, explicitly selected subset where selection is required, and final claims, with item-level lineage.
- **Accepted role-aligned outcome requirement:** `C0004-INTG-04` requires factual assertions and source-attribution errors to be scored separately from legitimate hypotheses, speculation, objections, and unresolved questions.
- **Accepted selection–use distinction:** `C0004-INTG-08` states that relevant evidence can be selected or received without being correctly attributed, qualified, or semantically used.
- **Accepted unresolved status:** The branch has no canonical evidence-item object, direct-support codebook, final-use definition, lineage codebook, evaluator protocol, or accepted evidence that this handoff contains enough stable direct-support pairs.
- **Rejected interpretation:** The selected handoff is not accepted as uniquely sensitive, representative, or best; it remains a pragmatic candidate subject to screening.

## Candidate Hypotheses and Possibilities

### C0005-EXP-01 — Measurable-pair sufficiency hypothesis

**Status: Hypothesis.** A bounded sample of External Evidence Researcher outputs may contain enough atomic factual claims paired with specific evidence excerpts or locators to support a reliable direct-support annotation exercise.

The relevant quantity is not total citations or source count. It is the number of candidate pairs that remain stable after applying explicit inclusion and exclusion rules and that can plausibly be traced into downstream Alternative Theorist work.

Distinguishing information would include candidate-pair prevalence, exclusion reasons, annotator agreement, and the number of pairs surviving identity and downstream-use checks.

### C0005-EXP-02 — Qualification-dominant handoff possibility

**Status: Hypothesis.** The most important relation at this handoff may not be simple claim → supporting evidence. It may instead be claim → scope or qualification, especially where downstream alternatives depend on limitations, uncertainty, population, task, resource, or transfer boundaries.

If direct-support pairs are sparse but qualification relations are frequent and reliably codable, the current relation choice may be a poor fit even though the handoff remains measurable in another relation class.

Distinguishing information would include the relative prevalence and reliability of direct-support, qualification, contradiction, and contextualization relations, without combining them into one primary estimand.

### C0005-EXP-03 — Identity-instability hypothesis

**Status: Hypothesis.** Claim identity may be the primary apparatus bottleneck. Alternative Theorist outputs can paraphrase, generalize, narrow, combine, split, or transform evidence-backed claims into hypotheses and objections, making one-to-one lineage rare.

Under this possibility, the evidence relation itself may be clear at the External Evidence Researcher stage while downstream use remains difficult to code because identity and epistemic status change.

Distinguishing information would include rates of exact preservation, paraphrase, split, merge, scope change, qualification weakening, support substitution, contradiction, and derived synthesis.

### C0005-EXP-04 — Selection–use dissociation hypothesis

**Status: Hypothesis.** A relation can be present in the written evidence package and even appear in a selected subset without affecting the semantic content of the Alternative Theorist output. Conversely, an Alternative Theorist may reconstruct and use a valid relation without explicitly selecting or citing the original pair.

This would imply that package presence, selected-subset inclusion, and final semantic use are distinct measurable checkpoints rather than interchangeable indicators.

Distinguishing information would include pair-level transitions across written, selected, and final-use states, including cases of selected-but-unused and unselected-but-reconstructed relations.

### C0005-EXP-05 — Sparse-reuse hypothesis

**Status: Hypothesis.** The Alternative Theorist role may legitimately generate alternatives, objections, and speculative mechanisms without reusing many External Evidence Researcher factual claims directly. If so, the target handoff may have low downstream factual-reuse density even when the role performs its function well.

This would not show that the role or handoff is ineffective. It would show that direct claim–support reuse is a weak primary measurement object for this transition.

Distinguishing information would include the proportion of Alternative Theorist units that are factual assertions, source interpretations, hypotheses, speculation, objections, or unresolved questions, and the fraction of factual units requiring direct upstream support.

### C0005-EXP-06 — Small reliable core possibility

**Status: Speculation.** A broad codebook may be unreliable, while a small subset of relations—such as atomic factual claim, exact supporting excerpt, source identifier, and preserved scope—may be coded consistently enough for an initial pilot.

This possibility is only speculative. It should not be treated as supported until a blinded reliability sample demonstrates that the retained class is both nontrivial and relevant to the downstream role.

### C0005-EXP-07 — Task-stratum concentration possibility

**Status: Speculation.** Measurable direct-support reuse may cluster in particular research tasks, source types, or claim forms rather than appearing evenly across the handoff.

A pooled average could therefore hide a usable stratum or make a narrow stratum appear generally representative. This is speculative and requires predeclared strata rather than post hoc selection.

## Possible Mechanisms or Connections

1. **Atomicity and identity connection:** Direct-support coding becomes easier when upstream claims are atomic and evidence units are specific. It becomes harder when claims bundle multiple propositions or when evidence is represented only at source level.

2. **Qualification preservation connection:** A downstream statement can preserve a central proposition while weakening its scope, uncertainty, or transfer limits. Semantic similarity alone may therefore overstate successful use.

3. **Epistemic transition connection:** A sourced fact may become a hypothesis premise, objection, or speculative mechanism downstream. Lineage needs to preserve both content identity and epistemic-status transition rather than treating every transformation as factual reuse.

4. **Many-to-many relation connection:** One claim may depend on multiple evidence units, and one evidence unit may support several claims. A minimal apparatus may need bounded many-to-many links even if the first reliability estimand uses a narrower one-claim/one-evidence subset.

5. **Write–select–use connection:** Failure can occur because a relation was not written, was written but not selected, was selected but not used, or was used with altered scope or attribution. One final error may have multiple contributing checkpoints.

6. **Role-fit connection:** The Alternative Theorist is authorized to generate hypotheses, speculation, objections, and unresolved questions. A measurement scheme centered only on factual reuse could misclassify productive role behavior as omission or unsupported output.

7. **Ecological-validity connection:** A highly restricted direct-support class may improve reliability while excluding the qualification-rich and synthesis-heavy relations most relevant to open-ended research. The screen should therefore report both reliability and retained coverage.

## Questions for Internal Evidence Research

1. Across an explicitly bounded sample of prior External Evidence Researcher outputs, how many candidate atomic claim–evidence pairs can be extracted under a provisional narrow direct-support rule?
2. What proportion of candidates must be excluded because the evidence unit is source-level only, derived summary, ambiguous, partial, contextual, qualification-only, contradictory, or many-to-many?
3. Which existing branch fields can populate a minimal object for claim ID, evidence-item ID, source ID, relation type, scope or qualification, and epistemic status without inventing unsupported semantics?
4. How often do candidate upstream claims appear downstream as exact preservation, paraphrase, split, merge, generalization, narrowing, scope change, qualification weakening, contradiction, or derived synthesis?
5. What fraction of Alternative Theorist output units are factual assertions or source interpretations that require upstream evidential support, versus hypotheses, speculation, objections, and unresolved questions?
6. Can downstream semantic use be identified from text alone, or are explicit pair references or an added selected-subset checkpoint required for observability?
7. How many candidate pairs survive all four screens: narrow relation class, stable identity, role-aligned downstream relevance, and codable final use?
8. Which task or claim strata show enough candidate density for a pilot without post hoc cherry-picking?
9. What disagreements arise between annotators, and do they concern relation type, claim atomicity, evidence boundaries, identity, epistemic transition, or final use?
10. What minimum retained coverage would prevent a reliable narrow codebook from becoming trivial or unrepresentative of the handoff?

## Questions for External Evidence Research

1. What primary studies or datasets define and reliability-test direct claim–evidence relations with explicit inclusion and exclusion rules?
2. What primary work distinguishes direct support from qualification, contextualization, contradiction, exemplification, motivation, or general relevance?
3. What methods exist for claim identity across paraphrase, split, merge, scope change, qualification change, and synthesis, and what limitations do those methods report?
4. What primary studies operationalize downstream semantic evidence use separately from citation presence, evidence selection, copied identifiers, or rationale generation?
5. What evidence exists on selection–use dissociation: evidence selected or displayed but not correctly used, or evidence used without explicit selection?
6. What annotation designs handle many-to-many claim–evidence relations and multi-cause error lineage without collapsing them into a single label?
7. What reliability statistics, adjudication procedures, blinding practices, and thresholds are used for complex relation and lineage annotation?
8. What primary evidence addresses the tradeoff between a narrow reliable relation codebook and ecological validity in long-form or open-ended synthesis?
9. Are there source-research → hypothesis-generation, evidence-collection → alternative-explanation, or closely analogous handoff studies that measure relation preservation and downstream use?
10. What evidence would justify switching the first relation class from direct support to qualification or another relation, rather than merely expanding the codebook?

## Potential Tests or Comparisons

### 1. Candidate prevalence screen

Take a predeclared bounded sample of External Evidence Researcher outputs and enumerate candidate atomic claim–evidence pairs. Report raw candidates, retained direct-support pairs, exclusions by reason, source and task strata, and downstream-relevant pairs.

This is a measurement screen, not an effect test.

### 2. Blinded duplicate codebook trial

Have independent annotators apply a provisional narrow codebook to the same candidate pairs without seeing downstream outcomes. Compare agreement on claim boundaries, evidence boundaries, direct-support status, scope or qualification, and exclusion reason.

A useful result would identify whether disagreement is concentrated in a small number of rules that can be clarified.

### 3. Identity stress test

For retained pairs, independently code whether downstream units preserve, paraphrase, split, merge, generalize, narrow, contradict, or synthesize the upstream claim. Compare simple semantic matching with relation- and status-aware human coding.

The goal is to determine whether stable lineage is feasible, not to select a preferred identity metric prematurely.

### 4. Write–select–use dry run

Using one common, explicitly costed selected-subset format, record whether each pair is present in the written package, included in the selected subset, and semantically used in the final output. Classify selected-but-unused, unselected-but-used, used-with-scope-change, and citation-only cases.

This dry run should use the same apparatus across any compared samples and should not be interpreted as natural latent retrieval.

### 5. Relation-class comparison

On the same predeclared sample, separately estimate prevalence and agreement for direct support, qualification, contradiction, and contextualization. Do not pool them. Compare which class yields the best combination of reliability, retained coverage, downstream relevance, and role fit.

### 6. Role-aligned unit coding

Segment Alternative Theorist outputs into factual assertion, source interpretation, hypothesis, speculation, objection, and unresolved question. Measure direct-support requirements only where epistemically appropriate, while separately recording alternative coverage and usefulness.

### 7. Measurability gate

Predeclare a gate using at least four dimensions: retained pair count, annotation reliability, identity-lineage reliability, and downstream-reuse prevalence. Failure on any dimension would trigger revision of the handoff, relation class, or schema rather than an effectiveness claim.

Exact thresholds remain unresolved and should be informed by Internal and External Evidence Research rather than invented here.

## Main Uncertainties

- The selected handoff may contain too few factual reuse events for direct-support relations to be a productive primary object.
- A narrow reliable codebook may retain too little of the handoff’s actual epistemic work.
- Claim identity may become ambiguous under legitimate alternative generation, especially split, merge, generalization, and epistemic-status change.
- Final semantic use may not be inferable without adding an explicit checkpoint that changes behavior and consumes resources.
- Direct support may be less relevant than qualification preservation for the branch objective.
- Many-to-many relations and multi-cause errors may defeat a simple pairwise lineage model.
- Apparent reliability may depend on task, source, claim form, reader model, or annotator expertise.
- Any successful measurability result would remain local to the sampled handoff and would not establish a relation benefit, role-separation benefit, fresh-invocation benefit, or full-loop superiority.

In particular, examine whether the proposed direct-support object, downstream semantic-use concept, relation-class comparison, and measurability gate can be specified without circularly selecting only easy pairs or encoding the evaluator’s preferred reasoning path.
