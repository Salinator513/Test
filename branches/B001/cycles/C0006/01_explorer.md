# Explorer — C0006

## Selected Frontier

Define a prospective denominator hierarchy and retained-coverage/role-relevance gate for the External Evidence Researcher → Alternative Theorist direct-support measurability pilot.

The narrow question is not whether direct-support coding can achieve high agreement in an eligible residue. It is whether the residue can be quantified against the full handoff workload so that a passing reliability result cannot hide trivial coverage, pair-count inflation, or exclusion of the Alternative Theorist’s central qualification-rich and synthesis-heavy work.

## Why This Frontier Now

The accepted branch state already requires a frozen development/audit split, a narrow direct-support primary relation, multidimensional observable incorporation coding, independent coding, metric-sensitive reliability, and predeclared failure actions. The strongest remaining objection is coverage and role mismatch: a highly reliable one-claim/one-evidence subset may be too small or unrepresentative to support the intended later experiment.

A denominator hierarchy is therefore a prerequisite for interpreting every later prevalence, reliability, coverage, and feasibility number. Without it, raw pair counts can be inflated by splitting, duplicate support, repeated citations, or many-to-many expansion, while exclusions can silently remove the most difficult and role-relevant material. This frontier is narrower than a complete pilot specification but would give the later roles a concrete basis for defining the corpus, sampling plan, gates, and failure actions.

## Relevant Accepted Branch State

- **Accepted methodological requirement:** Evaluation must separate atomic claims, support correctness, citation correctness, completeness, coverage, qualification, omission, usefulness, task completion, and stage lineage (`C0001-INTG-03`, `C0004-INTG-04`).
- **Accepted methodological requirement:** State-transfer and handoff studies need predeclared inventories, written packages, selected subsets where applicable, downstream units, and item-level lineage; none establishes latent causal use (`C0002-INTG-04`, `C0003-INTG-04`, `C0005-INTG-02`).
- **Accepted apparatus constraint:** The first audit may use a one-claim/one-evidence primary object only for an explicitly eligible subset, while bounded multi-unit and many-to-many structures and exclusion losses remain visible (`C0005-INTG-04`).
- **Accepted reliability constraint:** Reliability must be reported with prevalence, class-specific agreement, strict and relaxed boundaries, uncertainty, clustering, retained coverage, exclusion reasons, role relevance, and metric sensitivity (`C0005-INTG-05`).
- **Accepted direction:** Direct support remains the primary relation, with qualification preservation required; changing the primary relation requires a new prospective protocol and new held-out validation (`C0005-INTG-06`).
- **Accepted observational boundary:** A selected-subset checkpoint records selection only and is a costed intervention; visible packages cannot estimate write loss without a reference inventory (`C0005-INTG-07`).
- **Accepted competing possibilities:** A reliable small core, low coverage, identity instability, resource dependence, sparse factual reuse, and metric sensitivity may coexist (`C0005-INTG-09`).
- **Accepted failure-policy requirement:** Low prevalence, low reliability, low retained coverage, low role relevance, identity instability, metric sensitivity, or excessive resource dependence must trigger predeclared local actions rather than post hoc reinterpretation (`C0005-INTG-10`).
- **Unresolved branch questions:** The corpus, denominator levels, atomicity, deduplication, many-to-many rules, evidence boundaries, retained-coverage minimum, role-relevance minimum, clustering treatment, thresholds, and failure actions remain undefined.

## Candidate Hypotheses and Possibilities

### C0006-EXP-01 — Nontrivial eligible-core hypothesis

**Status:** Hypothesis.

A prospectively defined direct-support eligible subset may cover a nontrivial share of factual Alternative Theorist work after deduplication and clustering, while retaining enough qualification and scope information to be role relevant.

**Distinguishing information:** Counts and proportions at every denominator level; exclusion reasons; qualification density; task-stratum distribution; coder agreement; and the share of final outputs whose substantive factual content depends on eligible units.

### C0006-EXP-02 — Reliable-but-trivial residue hypothesis

**Status:** Hypothesis.

High reliability may be achievable only after excluding many-to-many, synthesis-heavy, qualification-dependent, boundary-unstable, or weakly localized cases, leaving a residue too small or too peripheral to justify a later handoff experiment.

**Distinguishing information:** Reliability plotted jointly with retained coverage and role relevance as exclusions tighten; composition of excluded material; and whether the retained subset represents central rather than incidental output content.

### C0006-EXP-03 — Denominator-inflation hypothesis

**Status:** Hypothesis.

Raw claim–evidence pair counts may substantially overstate measurable material because one upstream claim can link to multiple overlapping evidence units, one evidence unit can support multiple claims, and split/merge choices can multiply nominal pairs without increasing independent information.

**Distinguishing information:** Comparison of raw pairs, deduplicated relations, source-clustered relations, upstream-claim clusters, downstream-output clusters, and bounded many-to-many relation groups.

### C0006-EXP-04 — Factual-stratum concentration hypothesis

**Status:** Hypothesis.

Eligible direct-support relations may be concentrated in a prospectively identifiable factual stratum while being sparse in mechanism generation, objection generation, hybrid explanations, and unresolved-question work.

**Distinguishing information:** Predeclared role-function strata; denominator and eligibility rates within each stratum; output importance ratings; and whether a factual-stratum result can answer a useful later question without being generalized to the whole role.

### C0006-EXP-05 — Qualification-loss exclusion hypothesis

**Status:** Hypothesis.

A narrow direct-support rule may disproportionately exclude cases where the important relation is not bare support but preservation of scope, conditions, limitations, uncertainty, or contrast. The audit could therefore pass while missing the error modes most relevant to cumulative reasoning.

**Distinguishing information:** Qualification-bearing rate before eligibility filtering; reasons qualification-bearing cases are excluded; separate qualification-preservation coding among retained units; and comparison of retained versus excluded output importance.

### C0006-EXP-06 — Clustered-sparsity hypothesis

**Status:** Hypothesis.

Apparently adequate pair prevalence may collapse after accounting for repeated use of the same source, claim family, evidence passage, or downstream output segment. Effective sample diversity may be much lower than nominal pair count.

**Distinguishing information:** Counts by source, document, claim family, evidence passage, output section, task, and cycle; intracluster dependence estimates or sensitivity analyses; and the number of genuinely distinct relation groups.

### C0006-EXP-07 — Coverage-gate instability possibility

**Status:** Speculation.

A single scalar retained-coverage threshold may be unstable or misleading because the same percentage can represent many low-importance items or a few central items. A multidimensional gate may be required.

**Distinguishing information:** Item-count coverage, weighted substantive coverage, role-function coverage, qualification coverage, source diversity, and downstream-output coverage under the same eligibility rule.

### C0006-EXP-08 — Denominator observability limit

**Status:** Hypothesis.

Some desired denominators may not be recoverable from ordinary role outputs without adding structured checkpoints that alter behavior. The pilot may need to distinguish naturally observable denominators from intervention-created denominators.

**Distinguishing information:** A field-by-field map showing which denominator objects exist in ordinary artifacts, which require annotation only, and which require new agent reporting or selection checkpoints; separate resource and behavioral costs for each.

## Possible Mechanisms or Connections

1. **Atomicity and pair multiplication:** Finer splitting can increase nominal claim and pair counts, potentially improving apparent sample size while reducing natural semantic integrity. Coarser grouping can preserve synthesis but reduce boundary agreement.

2. **Many-to-many compression:** Treating a bounded relation group as one clustered object may better reflect the underlying dependency structure than expanding it into all pairwise edges. This may reduce count inflation but complicate reliability scoring.

3. **Eligibility filtering and ecological loss:** Restricting the primary audit to clean direct-support units can improve coding stability while selecting against ambiguity, qualification, synthesis, and cross-source integration.

4. **Role-function mismatch:** The Alternative Theorist is authorized to produce hypotheses, speculation, objections, hybrids, and unresolved questions. Direct-support prevalence should therefore be assessed only where assertive factual content is actually expected, while still measuring whether that stratum is important enough to justify the selected handoff.

5. **Importance weighting and circularity:** Weighting units by downstream importance could prevent trivial-item dominance, but importance rules must be frozen without using the audit outcomes or favoring units that happen to be easy to code.

6. **Clustering and pseudo-replication:** Multiple relations derived from the same source passage, claim family, or output paragraph may share the same underlying success or failure. Nominal pair-level reliability and precision can therefore overstate effective diversity.

7. **Checkpoint contamination:** Recording candidate relations or selected subsets can make denominators visible but may change attention, selection, and deliberation. Natural-output denominators and checkpoint-enabled denominators should not be treated as equivalent.

8. **Gate coupling:** Reliability, retained coverage, role relevance, qualification preservation, source diversity, and resource cost may trade off. A pass rule based on only one dimension could reward a narrow, expensive, or unrepresentative apparatus.

## Questions for Internal Evidence Research

1. Which exact denominator objects are already representable using accepted branch conventions without introducing new checkpoints: full handoff outputs, upstream factual units, evidence units, candidate relations, eligible relations, retained relations, downstream units, or output sections?

2. What branch-local definitions can distinguish an upstream factual claim from a hypothesis, speculation, objection, unresolved question, source interpretation, or derived conclusion at this handoff?

3. What prospective hierarchy would avoid double counting: corpus items → role outputs → output sections → upstream claim clusters → evidence clusters → bounded relation groups → eligible primary units → downstream incorporation units?

4. Which atomicity and deduplication rules can be derived from accepted branch distinctions, and which remain entirely new design choices?

5. How should split, merge, paraphrase, support substitution, repeated citation, repeated source use, and many-to-many structures alter the denominator rather than merely the lineage labels?

6. What observable fields are necessary to classify every excluded candidate and preserve an auditable exclusion ledger?

7. Which predeclared role-function strata are justified by the protocol and current accepted state, and which would be post hoc inventions?

8. Can role relevance be operationalized using observable output function and substantive importance without asking whether the relation treatment later improves performance?

9. Which denominator levels require full-source access, independent coding, adjudication, or added structured reporting, and how should those resource costs be recorded?

10. What minimal frozen data schema would let later agents compute raw prevalence, deduplicated prevalence, cluster-adjusted prevalence, retained coverage, qualification coverage, and role-function coverage without reopening identity rules?

## Questions for External Evidence Research

1. What primary methodological sources define denominator hierarchies for nested or clustered annotation objects rather than treating every pair as independent?

2. What primary sources provide methods for reporting retained coverage jointly with reliability after eligibility filtering or abstention?

3. What primary sources address pseudo-replication or dependence when many labeled relations share a document, passage, claim, source, participant, or output segment?

4. What primary sources compare pairwise expansion with grouped, hyperedge, set-valued, or many-to-many relation annotation?

5. What primary sources offer prospective sampling or audit designs that preserve rare but substantively important strata without selecting favorable strata after inspection?

6. What primary sources distinguish item-count coverage from weighted content coverage, task coverage, or representativeness in annotation and evaluation systems?

7. What primary sources tie feasibility thresholds to the intended downstream decision, false-passage cost, and precision requirements rather than supplying universal agreement cutoffs?

8. What primary sources describe exclusion ledgers, abstention analysis, or selective-prediction-style coverage–quality curves that could be adapted without claiming direct transfer?

9. What primary sources address reliability for hierarchical units, overlapping spans, split/merge identities, and bounded many-to-many relations?

10. Is there a direct precedent for testing whether a highly reliable annotated subset remains representative of a role’s full functional workload, especially when factual and nonfactual outputs coexist?

## Potential Tests or Comparisons

1. **Denominator reconciliation test:** Apply two independently drafted denominator hierarchies to the same development sample and compare counts, deduplication decisions, many-to-many grouping, exclusions, and resulting coverage estimates. Large divergence would indicate that the denominator object is not yet frozen enough for audit.

2. **Atomicity sensitivity comparison:** Recompute prevalence and coverage under predeclared coarse and fine atomicity rules. The goal is not to choose the rule with better results but to identify whether feasibility conclusions reverse under plausible boundary choices.

3. **Pair-versus-group comparison:** Compare nominal pair counts with bounded relation-group counts and cluster-adjusted effective counts. This tests whether apparent prevalence is mostly pair expansion.

4. **Coverage–reliability frontier:** On development data only, chart reliability against retained item coverage, qualification coverage, role-function coverage, and substantive-output coverage as eligibility restrictions change. Freeze one rule before held-out audit.

5. **Exclusion-composition audit:** Require every excluded candidate to receive one or more predeclared exclusion codes. Compare retained and excluded material by role function, qualification density, source count, synthesis complexity, and output importance.

6. **Stratum stability comparison:** Predeclare a small number of protocol-grounded strata and test whether denominator rules and eligibility judgments remain stable across them. Do not promote a favorable stratum after audit inspection.

7. **Clustering sensitivity analysis:** Report results under pair-level, upstream-claim-cluster, evidence-passage-cluster, source-cluster, and downstream-output-cluster analyses. A feasibility claim should state which level supports it.

8. **Natural-versus-checkpoint observability comparison:** On development material, compare denominators recoverable from ordinary artifacts with denominators recoverable only after a structured candidate-relation or selection report. Record the additional behavioral and resource intervention.

9. **Importance-weighting robustness test:** Compare unweighted coverage with a prospectively defined importance-weighted measure whose rubric is blind to relation eligibility and audit outcome. Divergence would show whether easy peripheral units dominate the count.

10. **False-passage simulation:** Before opening held-out audit data, specify hypothetical outcomes such as high reliability/low coverage, moderate reliability/high coverage, stratum-specific passage, or high cost. Confirm that each maps to a predetermined action rather than interpretive flexibility.

## Main Uncertainties

- No authorized development or audit corpus is yet defined, so all hypotheses remain untested.
- The branch has not fixed the natural top-level denominator: role outputs, factual output units, candidate evidence relations, or bounded relation groups.
- Atomicity and deduplication rules may materially change prevalence and retained coverage.
- Role relevance may require weighting, but weighting can introduce evaluator preference or circularity.
- Many-to-many structure may resist reduction to a one-claim/one-evidence reliability unit without losing the target phenomenon.
- Some denominator levels may require checkpoints that change behavior and consume resources.
- A factual stratum may be measurable but too narrow to justify claims about the full Alternative Theorist role.
- High reliability may coexist with low qualification coverage, low source diversity, or low substantive-output coverage.
- Nominal sample size may be misleading under source, claim, evidence, and output clustering.
- No accepted numeric threshold currently defines sufficient prevalence, retained coverage, role relevance, reliability, precision, diversity, or cost.

## Handoff to Critic

Assess whether the proposed denominator-and-coverage frontier is sufficiently narrow, noncircular, and prior to codebook validation. In particular, test whether the hierarchy can avoid pair inflation without replacing the natural many-to-many object, whether role relevance can be defined prospectively without outcome-based weighting, whether a factual stratum would answer a meaningful branch question, and whether any required checkpoint would invalidate claims about the ordinary handoff.
