# External Evidence Researcher — C0006

## Research Scope

This report addresses the C0006 frontier as narrowed by the Critic and Internal Evidence Researcher: methods for constructing a **linked denominator-and-observability system** for the External Evidence Researcher → Alternative Theorist handoff, and the conditions under which a later retained-coverage or role-relevance gate could be specified. It does not estimate B001 prevalence, reliability, representativeness, role relevance, or a numeric pass threshold.

The search was restricted to methods that bear directly on: non-nested or cross-classified objects; edge-level and grouped relation representations; candidate universes generated by a bounded procedure; retained coverage after filtering or abstention; clustered dependence and pseudo-replication; prospective stratification and outcome-independent weighting; split/merge and overlapping identity; exclusion accounting; and decision-linked reliability or feasibility thresholds.

The main direction evaluated here is not that any particular denominator lattice is already valid. It is the narrower proposition that B001 should preserve distinct objects and mappings, report quality jointly with retained coverage and exclusions, and delay a numeric gate until the downstream decision, population, sampling unit, and costs are fixed.

No located source directly studies a role-separated research loop, the selected B001 handoff, a mixed factual/nonfactual Alternative Theorist workload, or fixed-inference-compute role separation. External methods are therefore classified below as directly reusable components, bounded methodological precedents, or analogies.

## Search Questions

1. What primary methods represent observations that belong to multiple, crossed, or non-nested object classes?
2. What methods preserve both individual relation edges and grouped, set-valued, entity-level, or many-to-many structure?
3. How can candidate universes be defined by a reproducible generation procedure when the latent universe of true relations is unknown?
4. What methods report quality or error jointly with retained coverage, rejection, abstention, or exclusions?
5. What methods address dependence when annotations share sources, documents, passages, claims, outputs, tasks, or annotators?
6. What methods handle overlapping boundaries, unitization, split/merge identity, and set-valued annotation?
7. What prospective sampling and weighting methods preserve rare or substantively important strata without post hoc subgroup promotion?
8. What reporting precedents keep exclusions visible rather than treating filtered material as nonexistent?
9. What methods tie sample size or operating thresholds to a downstream decision, error cost, precision, power, or false-passage consequence?
10. Is there direct evidence that a highly reliable retained subset remains representative of a mixed functional workload?

## Search Method and Source Hierarchy

Searches targeted original papers, official proceedings, institutional publication records, and canonical DOI pages. Priority was given to peer-reviewed methodological papers and original preprints; official system documentation and reporting standards were retained only when they supplied a missing apparatus component. Secondary descriptions were used only to locate canonical sources and were not used as evidence. No forum or social-media statement was used to confirm a factual claim.

The retained ledger contains **20 original or official primary sources**. They fall into partially dependent families: structured annotation and relation evaluation; TREC pooling; selective prediction and rejection; clustered or crossed inference; probability sampling and weighting; decision-linked reliability; reporting and observability. Source count is not an independence count.

The review deliberately excluded another general survey of fact verification, citation attribution, qualification coding, or factuality metrics because C0005 already covered those areas. A source from an adjacent field was retained only when it supplied a denominator, candidate-universe, coverage, clustering, sampling, exclusion, or decision-threshold method absent from the accepted record.

For transfer assessment, “directly reusable” means the mathematical or data-structural method can be instantiated on B001 objects after B001-specific definitions are supplied. It does not mean that the source validates the B001 construct or threshold. “Analogy” means the source establishes a general design principle but its objects, decisions, or sampling assumptions differ materially.

## Evidence Supporting the Main Direction

### C0006-EXT-01 — Non-nested objects require explicit crossed or multiple-membership mappings

Browne, Goldstein, and Rasbash (`C0006-SRC-01`) show that complex observations need not fit a single nesting hierarchy: classifications can be crossed, and one observation can belong to multiple higher-level units. Their examples span education, veterinary epidemiology, demography, and public health. This is a direct methodological precedent for treating B001 task instances, outputs, claims, evidence units, relation groups, downstream units, and clusters as linked object classes rather than forcing them into one denominator ladder.

The source supports a lattice or multi-table representation and an inferential model that names the relevant memberships. It does not tell B001 which mappings are semantically valid, which object should be the primary sampling unit, or which numerator may be divided by which denominator. Those choices still require a frozen target decision and B001 codebook.

### C0006-EXT-02 — Complex relation structure can be represented without pairwise flattening

The brat paper and official representation (`C0006-SRC-02`) provide a concrete annotation system capable of text-bound annotations, discontinuous spans, binary relations, equivalence classes, attributes, and n-ary event-like structures. This establishes implementation feasibility for preserving relation edges, grouped argument structures, and multi-span evidence objects in one data model.

Passonneau’s MASI method (`C0006-SRC-03`) treats an annotation item as a set and defines a graded set-distance for inter-annotator agreement. The paper reports reliability for five pairs of independently created summarization pyramids from DUC 2003. MASI is directly reusable for bounded set-valued fields, such as the members of a relation group or a multi-span evidence object, provided the set universe and semantic meaning are fixed.

Mathet, Widlöcher, and Métivier’s gamma method (`C0006-SRC-04`) jointly addresses unitizing and categorization by aligning annotation units before computing agreement. It is a direct precedent for boundary-sensitive reliability when coders can place different spans or segment the same material differently. It supports reporting strict and relaxed or distance-sensitive results rather than assuming presegmented identical units.

Luo’s CEAF (`C0006-SRC-05`) evaluates structured coreference entities through an optimal one-to-one alignment between reference and system entity clusters. Moosavi and Strube’s LEA (`C0006-SRC-06`) instead combines entity importance with link resolution and documents serious interpretability problems in several established coreference metrics. Together they support keeping edge/link and group/entity views visible in parallel. They also show that a grouping metric encodes a substantive theory of identity and error; it cannot be treated as a neutral count reduction.

### C0006-EXT-03 — Candidate universes should be named by their generation procedure

TREC pooling is a strong direct precedent for a bounded candidate universe. Buckley, Dimmick, Soboroff, and Voorhees (`C0006-SRC-07`) analyze test collections in which the judged document set is created by pooling documents retrieved by participating systems. The pool is a procedural universe, not the latent set of every relevant document. Their analysis of the AQUAINT/TREC 2005 setting found that a fixed pool depth could be inadequate for a much larger collection and could introduce bias.

Voorhees, Soboroff, and Lin (`C0006-SRC-08`) later tested the reusability of the TREC-8 collection by obtaining new judgments for pools formed from five new runs—three transformer-based systems and two BM25 baselines—plus previously unpooled TREC-8 submissions. Rankings under the original and expanded judgment sets were almost identical, with Kendall correlations above 0.99. This supports a more qualified rule: a procedurally incomplete universe can be adequate for a stated evaluation decision when the pool is deep, diverse, and empirically stress-tested.

For B001, writer-supplied links, coder-proposed links under fixed source access, all links within a bounded evidence set, and checkpoint-reported candidates should therefore remain separate universes. No source supports labeling any of them “all possible true relations.” Pool depth, contributing procedures, source access, and later challenge sets must be recorded.

### C0006-EXT-04 — Quality must be reported jointly with retained coverage

Chow’s reject-option analysis (`C0006-SRC-09`) formally links recognition error and rejection probability under an optimal decision rule. Geifman and El-Yaniv (`C0006-SRC-10`) adapt selective classification to trained deep networks, allowing a user to set a target risk and reject test instances to achieve that risk with high probability. On CIFAR and ImageNet, they demonstrate the central tradeoff: lower reported error is obtained by covering fewer items. Their ImageNet example reports approximately 2% top-5 error with about 60% coverage at the stated confidence level.

SelectiveNet (`C0006-SRC-11`) jointly trains prediction and rejection and reports improved risk–coverage tradeoffs across standard classification and regression datasets. These sources directly support a coverage–quality curve and explicitly weaken any practice that reports reliability or accuracy only on the retained residue.

Transfer is limited. Predictive risk is not inter-annotator reliability, and a selector optimized with the predictor is an intervention that can change the retained distribution. For B001, the analogous plot should show agreement or validity together with named retained-coverage measures, but it cannot inherit the selective-classification guarantees or operating thresholds.

### C0006-EXT-05 — Nominal pair counts are not independent evidence when units share higher-level causes

Hurlbert (`C0006-SRC-12`) defines pseudoreplication as using inferential statistics where replicates are not independent or the error term does not match the treatment replication. His review of 176 ecological field studies found pseudoreplication in 27% overall and 48% of studies using inferential statistics. The domain differs, but the design warning transfers directly: several relation edges sharing a source passage, claim family, task, or output cannot automatically be counted as independent replicates for a claim generalized at one of those higher levels.

Barr, Levy, Scheepers, and Tily (`C0006-SRC-13`) use theory and Monte Carlo simulation to argue that confirmatory mixed-effects analyses should include random effects justified by the experimental design, including subject and item variation. This supports declaring the intended population and modeling the dependence structure implied by the design rather than selecting a convenient cluster level after observing results.

The practical implication for B001 is not “always use the smallest count” or “always fit a maximal model.” It is that pair-level, source-level, claim-family-level, output-level, and task-level analyses answer different generalization questions. The primary cluster or random-effect structure must follow the intended later inference; other structures can be frozen sensitivity analyses.

### C0006-EXT-06 — Rare-stratum preservation and weighted coverage require a sampling frame and prospective inclusion rules

Neyman (`C0006-SRC-15`) distinguishes probability-based stratified sampling from purposive selection and provides the foundation for allocating samples across predeclared strata. Horvitz and Thompson (`C0006-SRC-16`) provide an estimator for unequal-probability samples from a finite universe using known inclusion probabilities.

These are directly reusable only if B001 defines a finite sampling frame, freezes role-function or task strata before audit inspection, and records nonzero inclusion probabilities. They support oversampling rare but important strata for precision while using design weights to recover a population-level estimate. They do not validate any proposed role-function taxonomy or importance weight.

A key safeguard follows: sampling weights should be determined by the sampling design, not by later eligibility, reliability, or treatment results. Separately, any substantive-importance weight is a different construct and should be frozen blind to apparatus outcomes. Weighted and unweighted coverage must both remain visible.

### C0006-EXT-07 — Reliability sample size and operating thresholds should be decision-linked, not universal

Walter, Eliasziw, and Donner (`C0006-SRC-17`) derive sample-size and design requirements for intraclass-correlation reliability studies from a null reliability, target reliability, number of raters, significance level, and power. This is a direct precedent for specifying what reliability difference the audit must distinguish and with what uncertainty. It does not provide a universal acceptable ICC and does not directly apply to B001’s clustered, multilabel, boundary-sensitive objects.

Vickers and Elkin’s decision-curve analysis (`C0006-SRC-18`) evaluates prediction models using net benefit across threshold probabilities that encode the relative consequences of false-positive and false-negative decisions. The method is an analogy for a B001 false-passage policy: a gate should authorize a stated downstream action only when the value of passing and the costs of false passage and false failure are specified.

Together these sources support the Critic’s correction. A numerical gate cannot be justified from a generic agreement cutoff. The later experiment, claim scope, population, unit of inference, minimum meaningful reliability, uncertainty, and consequences of an erroneous pass must be fixed first.

### C0006-EXT-08 — Exclusions should remain in a flow and reason ledger

The CONSORT 2010 Statement (`C0006-SRC-19`) requires transparent reporting of participant flow, numbers analyzed, losses, exclusions, and reasons. Its object is a randomized trial rather than an annotation audit, so it is an analogy rather than validation of a B001 schema. Nevertheless, it supplies a strong reporting precedent: exclusions are part of the accountable study flow and should not disappear from the denominator narrative.

For B001, every object in a bounded candidate universe should retain an exclusion record with reason codes, affected role function, qualification burden, source structure, checkpoint dependence, and the stage at which exclusion occurred. This supports ecological-loss reporting but does not decide whether an exclusion is substantively acceptable.

### C0006-EXT-09 — Reported rationales or checkpoints do not identify latent causal dependence

Turpin, Michael, Perez, and Bowman (`C0006-SRC-20`) test chain-of-thought explanations from GPT-3.5 and Claude 1.0 under biasing input features across multiple-choice reasoning and social-bias settings. Model answers can be strongly affected by the biasing feature while the generated explanation fails to disclose that influence. This is direct evidence against treating a model’s report or rationale as a faithful causal account of its decision process.

The paper does not test B001 selection checkpoints or evidence-lineage reports. Its transfer is therefore limited but relevant: candidate reports, selected-subset declarations, citations, or rationales can provide observable records, yet they cannot establish latent retrieval, causal use, or counterfactual dependence. A checkpoint-created denominator must also be labeled as an intervention-created record.

## Evidence Weakening the Main Direction

### C0006-EXT-10 — No direct precedent validates representativeness of the retained B001 subset

No located source tests whether a reliably annotated direct-support subset remains representative of the full functional workload of an agent authorized to produce factual assertions, source interpretations, derived conclusions, hypotheses, speculation, objections, hybrids, and unresolved questions. The closest sources concern classification coverage, information-retrieval judgment pools, summarization pyramids, coreference entities, clinical decisions, or experimental sampling.

The external record therefore supports methods for making loss visible, not a claim that the proposed factual stratum is sufficiently central or representative. B001 role relevance remains a construct to be defined prospectively and validated locally.

### C0006-EXT-11 — A linked lattice is descriptive until a sampling and inferential model is fixed

Cross-classified and multiple-membership models show how to analyze non-nested data when memberships and a stochastic model are specified. They do not make every cross-object ratio valid. A lattice can prevent category errors, but it cannot by itself establish the target population, mapping direction, cluster unit, estimand, or weighting rule.

The denominator map and allowed-ratio table remain co-dependent on atomicity, identity, evidence-boundary, direct-support, split/merge, duplicate, complementary-support, substitutable-support, and many-to-many rules. External evidence does not support freezing denominator counts independently of the minimal codebook.

### C0006-EXT-12 — Grouping removes some inflation but can erase legitimate multiplicity

MASI, CEAF, LEA, and gamma do not converge on one universally correct treatment of structured disagreement. MASI treats values as sets; CEAF aligns entity clusters one-to-one; LEA scores links while weighting entities; gamma aligns units under a chosen dissimilarity. Each metric emphasizes different errors.

This weakens any claim that relation-group count is inherently more truthful than edge count. Several passages may supply complementary components; several claims may genuinely share one passage; split claims may preserve real scope or qualification differences. B001 should retain edge-level and group-level objects and report sensitivity to the grouping rule rather than replacing one count with the other.

### C0006-EXT-13 — Coverage–quality curves do not determine a valid operating point

Selective classification demonstrates that quality can improve as coverage falls, but it does not tell B001 how much role workload may be discarded. Geifman and El-Yaniv’s 2017 and 2019 studies are also one closely related author and method family, not two independent validations. SelectiveNet jointly optimizes the selector and predictor, so its retained subset is treatment-dependent rather than a passive sample.

A B001 coverage–reliability frontier can reveal a tradeoff on development data. It cannot establish role relevance, ecological validity, or the correct audit threshold without a decision target and false-passage cost.

### C0006-EXT-14 — Deep procedural pools can be robust, but only after expensive challenge testing

Buckley et al. document pooling bias when the judgment process is too shallow for the collection. Voorhees et al. find that the deeply pooled, diverse, and expensive TREC-8 collection remains highly reusable against later neural runs. The apparent conflict is informative: incompleteness is not automatically fatal, and a high-quality procedural universe is not automatically representative.

The robust result required new retrieval runs, new pools, assessor judgments, and comparison against an expanded set. Transferred to B001, a bounded candidate universe may be defensible only after prospective challenge procedures and resource accounting. The source does not support assuming writer-supplied or coder-proposed links are exhaustive.

### C0006-EXT-15 — Cluster modeling has no universal off-the-shelf specification

Barr et al. argue for the maximal random-effects structure justified by design. Bates, Kliegl, Vasishth, and Baayen (`C0006-SRC-14`) show that maximal mixed models can be overparameterized, fail to converge, and become uninterpretable when data do not support the complexity; they propose diagnostics and principled simplification.

This conflict weakens any universal rule such as “cluster at every visible level” or “use one effective sample size.” B001 needs a declared generalization target, adequate numbers at each level, diagnostics, and a frozen fallback policy. Pair, passage, source, output, and task clustering may all be useful sensitivity dimensions, but none is automatically the primary answer.

### C0006-EXT-16 — Stratification and inverse-probability weighting fail without a frame

Neyman and Horvitz–Thompson methods assume a specified finite population or sampling frame, prospective strata or inclusion probabilities, and a probability sampling design. B001 currently lacks an authorized corpus, a target population, a validated role-function taxonomy, and inclusion probabilities.

These methods therefore cannot rescue a convenience sample or convert post hoc favorable strata into representative evidence. If some units have zero or unknown inclusion probability, population recovery is not identified. Importance ratings are not substitutes for sampling weights.

### C0006-EXT-17 — Exclusion transparency does not validate exclusions

CONSORT-style flow reporting makes losses visible, but a complete exclusion ledger can coexist with severe ecological loss. It is a reporting safeguard, not evidence that the retained residue is representative, reliable, or useful. Similarly, brat shows that complex structures can be serialized, not that annotators can identify them consistently.

The apparatus must therefore report both the reasoned exclusions and the retained workload dimensions. High schema completeness or transparent bookkeeping cannot substitute for coverage and role-relevance evidence.

## Evidence About Alternative Explanations

1. **Apparatus-induced selection:** SelectiveNet shows that optimizing a rejection mechanism changes the retained distribution. A B001 eligibility rule, selected-subset checkpoint, or coder-generated candidate universe can likewise change exposure and composition rather than merely measure a natural subset.

2. **Resource privilege:** The robust TREC-8 result depended on deep, diverse pools and substantial assessor cost; the 2022 stress test added new runs and judgments. Apparent candidate-universe adequacy may be caused by expensive curation and challenge testing rather than by the denominator representation itself.

3. **Metric-induced feasibility:** CEAF, LEA, MASI, and gamma encode different identity, alignment, link, set, and boundary choices. A pass under one metric can fail under another without any change in the underlying annotations.

4. **Sampling-frame privilege:** Stratification and inverse-probability weighting can preserve rare strata only when a target frame and inclusion process exist. A favorable weighted result can otherwise reflect an invented population or outcome-informed weights.

5. **Cluster-model choice:** Hurlbert, Barr et al., and Bates et al. show that dependence must be modeled but that model structure and complexity matter. Cluster collapse can reflect true dependence, an overly coarse grouping rule, or an overfit random-effects model.

6. **Role-function composition:** External methods focus on units expected to have a correct label or prediction. They do not resolve whether sparse direct support is a failure or a legitimate consequence of an Alternative Theorist role dominated by hypotheses, objections, and unresolved questions.

7. **Checkpoint-report unfaithfulness:** Turpin et al. show that generated explanations can omit factors that influenced answers. A rationale or selected-set report may improve observability while remaining an incomplete or post hoc account.

8. **Decision-rule construction:** Walter et al. and Vickers–Elkin make thresholds conditional on hypotheses, power, preferences, and costs. A gate can appear rigorous while merely encoding unexamined choices about false passage, false failure, and the value of the later experiment.

## Fixed-Compute Versus Added-Compute Evidence

None of the retained sources compares role-separated and repeated single-agent research under matched inference compute. None isolates a fresh-conversation boundary, role separation, or a seven-agent architecture.

The methodological gains in the ledger generally require additional or differently allocated resources:

- brat requires schema design and annotation interaction;
- MASI, gamma, CEAF, and LEA require segmentation, alignment, grouping, or distance computation;
- TREC pooling requires retrieval runs, pooled candidates, assessor judgments, and in the 2022 study, new challenge runs and rejudging;
- Geifman and El-Yaniv’s methods require calibration or joint selective training and reject a portion of test items;
- crossed or mixed-effects models require sufficient observations per facet and additional modeling decisions;
- stratified sampling requires a frame, allocation design, and design weights;
- reliability planning requires raters, repeated measurements, and a target power calculation;
- exclusion ledgers and flow diagrams require complete tracking;
- rationale or checkpoint collection adds tokens and can alter behavior.

Therefore, no source supports treating a cleaner retained subset as an equal-resource result. A fixed retained-item count is not a fixed full-resource comparison. B001 would need to charge corpus construction, source access, candidate generation, annotation, grouping, adjudication, checkpoint reporting, statistical modeling, challenge testing, and evaluation, as well as model tokens and calls.

The selective-prediction papers are especially important for interpretation. Their improved error on covered items is achieved by rejecting items, and SelectiveNet additionally changes training. This is a coverage and resource tradeoff, not evidence that role separation improves reasoning at fixed compute.

## Conflicting or Inconclusive Findings

- **Pooling bias versus pooling robustness:** Buckley et al. find that shallow pooling can bias large-collection evaluation; Voorhees et al. find that a deep and diverse TREC-8 pool remains robust to later neural systems. Pool adequacy is conditional on depth, diversity, corpus scale, target systems, judgment budget, and challenge testing.

- **Maximal versus parsimonious dependence models:** Barr et al. favor maximal design-justified random effects for confirmatory generalization; Bates et al. warn that maximal models can be unsupported, nonconvergent, or uninterpretable. The external record does not identify a universal B001 cluster model.

- **Entity alignment versus link-aware scoring:** CEAF and LEA operationalize structured error differently, and Moosavi–Strube document pathologies in established metrics. No single metric is externally established as appropriate for B001 split/merge and many-to-many lineage.

- **Post-hoc rejection versus jointly learned selection:** The 2017 selective classifier operates on a trained network, while SelectiveNet learns selection and prediction together. Both produce risk–coverage curves, but they estimate different interventions.

- **Reliability versus retained representativeness:** MASI and gamma can measure structured agreement, and selective prediction can measure quality at coverage, but no source joins these with a validated mixed-role workload representativeness construct.

- **Weighted versus unweighted coverage:** Horvitz–Thompson weights can recover a finite-population estimand under known inclusion probabilities. They do not justify substantive importance weights, and no source validates outcome-blind importance weighting for an Alternative Theorist workload.

- **Ordinary-artifact versus intervention-created observability:** Turpin et al. weaken faith in generated explanations as causal records, but no located primary source directly compares the same agentic handoff with and without a structured candidate, selection, or rationale checkpoint while holding other conditions fixed.

## Source Reliability and Independence

The strongest sources for the immediate frontier are original methodological papers or official proceedings. Their relevance is high for the apparatus component they define but usually indirect for B001’s role workload.

- `C0006-SRC-01` supplies a general statistical framework for crossed and multiple memberships; it is not an annotation validation.
- `C0006-SRC-02` through `C0006-SRC-06` form a structured-annotation/evaluation family. They are not independent evidence that one B001 codebook will be reliable.
- `C0006-SRC-07` and `C0006-SRC-08` share the TREC/NIST evaluation tradition, overlapping authors, and related collections. Their disagreement is a within-family boundary test, not independent replication across domains.
- `C0006-SRC-09` through `C0006-SRC-11` are one reject-option/selective-prediction lineage; the two Geifman–El-Yaniv papers share authors and conceptual machinery.
- `C0006-SRC-12` through `C0006-SRC-14` address dependence from ecology and psycholinguistic/statistical modeling. They provide general design principles and an important methodological conflict, not direct annotation results.
- `C0006-SRC-15` and `C0006-SRC-16` are foundational finite-population sampling methods. Their validity depends on assumptions currently absent in B001.
- `C0006-SRC-17` and `C0006-SRC-18` tie design or thresholding to stated hypotheses and consequences, but their outcome models differ from B001.
- `C0006-SRC-19` is an official reporting guideline and `C0006-SRC-20` is a peer-reviewed model-behavior experiment. Each supports one narrow precaution.

The ledger should not be interpreted as 20 independent confirmations of a denominator lattice. It is a component-method ledger spanning distinct but sometimes related problems.

## Evidence Gaps

1. No direct study tests a linked denominator lattice for an External Evidence Researcher → Alternative Theorist handoff.
2. No source defines the target population of mixed factual, interpretive, hypothetical, speculative, objection, hybrid, and unresolved-question work.
3. No source validates a role-relevance rubric for this workload or an importance weight blind to eligibility and audit outcomes.
4. No B001-authorized development corpus, held-out audit corpus, task frame, or nonzero inclusion-probability design exists.
5. No exhaustive latent candidate-relation universe is observable; every practical universe is procedure-bounded.
6. No external method determines B001 atomicity, split/merge identity, duplicate versus complementary support, evidence sufficiency, qualification preservation, or bounded many-to-many semantics.
7. No source establishes which cluster—task, output, source, passage, claim family, relation group, downstream unit, or annotator—matches the intended B001 generalization target.
8. No source supplies a universal agreement, retained-coverage, role-relevance, diversity, or cost threshold suitable for B001.
9. No study estimates how a candidate, selection, or rationale checkpoint changes the ordinary handoff’s behavior and compute while preserving the same task and model conditions.
10. No retained source isolates role separation, fresh invocation, or full-loop architecture from added information, annotation, curation, selection, and compute.
11. No source shows that a high-reliability factual residue preserves qualification-rich, synthesis-heavy, or nonfactual central work.
12. No source resolves how to combine weighted and unweighted coverage, edge and group counts, or multiple clustering sensitivities into one pass decision without a predeclared downstream utility model.

## Handoff to Alternative Theorist and Integrator

The strongest externally supported direction is to treat the denominator system as a linked set of object classes with explicit candidate-generation procedures and mapping rules, preserve edge and grouped-relation views, and report reliability or validity jointly with retained coverage, exclusions, and dependence-sensitive uncertainty. This is supported by crossed/multiple-membership modeling, structured-annotation metrics, TREC pooling, selective prediction, sampling theory, and decision-linked reliability methods.

The strongest weakening result is that no source validates a numeric B001 gate or the representativeness of a reliable direct-support residue for the full Alternative Theorist workload. Every imported method requires assumptions that B001 has not fixed: a target decision, population, sampling frame, candidate universe, identity rules, grouping semantics, primary cluster, role-relevance rubric, and false-passage cost.

The Alternative Theorist should therefore consider at least these live accounts without selecting a winner:

1. A linked lattice may prevent invalid ratios while still leaving the substantive object underidentified.
2. A reliable small core may be real but role-peripheral, qualification-poor, or cluster-concentrated.
3. Apparatus and candidate-generation procedures may create the apparent measurable core.
4. Deep curation and challenge testing may make a bounded universe adequate at high resource cost.
5. Metric, grouping, weighting, and cluster choices may dominate pass/fail conclusions.
6. A factual-stratum pilot may be useful only if the later decision and claim are explicitly limited to that stratum.
7. A rationale or selection checkpoint may improve observable lineage without identifying natural retrieval or causal use.

For integration, the external evidence supports a **denominator-map deliverable now** and only a **conditional gate specification later**. No universal threshold, role-relevance conclusion, fixed-compute architecture claim, or fresh-role effect should be accepted from this ledger.
