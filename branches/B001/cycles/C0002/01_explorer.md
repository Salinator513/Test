# Explorer — C0002

## Selected Frontier

Operationalize **informational equivalence and state completeness** for the primary fresh-boundary isolation contrast: compare one persistent staged workflow with fresh staged invocations while holding the stage sequence, evidence, intermediate messages, artifacts, verifier inputs, candidate opportunities, selector rules, and final integration access as nearly identical as the protocol can define.

The narrow question is not whether the full seven-role loop is generally superior. It is whether a fresh invocation boundary has any measurable effect when the information crossing each stage boundary is controlled, and whether that effect changes as the carried state becomes more or less complete.

## Why This Frontier Now

C0001 accepted that the current evidence does not establish a general independent benefit from fresh role separation (`C0001-INTG-01`) and that the reviewed evidence does not fully isolate fresh role separation from added or differently allocated compute (`C0001-INTG-02`). The strongest unresolved objection is that fresh and persistent workflows have not been made informationally and computationally equivalent.

This frontier is useful now because informational equivalence is a prerequisite for interpreting the branch’s proposed primary contrast. Without a declared rule for what information is preserved, omitted, transformed, or exposed at each stage, any observed difference could be attributed to context reset, interface loss, state compression, hidden access to earlier reasoning, or unequal verifier information rather than to the invocation boundary itself.

The frontier also directly develops the accepted but unresolved fresh-context-plus-state interaction hypothesis: fresh execution may reduce contamination only when external state preserves the dependencies required for later correction and integration.

## Relevant Accepted Branch State

- **Accepted branch-design fact:** B001 bundles ordered functional roles, fresh conversations, role-specific permissions, externalized state, explicit epistemic categories, exact-reference requirements, and stage-gated progression. These are treatment features, not evidence of improved accuracy.
- **Accepted derived conclusion (`C0001-INTG-01`):** Current evidence supports conditional architecture fit rather than a general independent fresh-role advantage.
- **Accepted evidence-gap conclusion (`C0001-INTG-02`):** The C0001 ledger does not fully isolate fresh role separation from added or differently allocated compute.
- **Accepted methodological requirement (`C0001-INTG-03`):** Evaluation must be claim-level, coverage-sensitive, and capable of tracing errors across stages as introduced, preserved, corrected, mutated, or amplified.
- **Qualified risk (`C0001-EXP-05`):** Narrow contexts, permission boundaries, and handoffs may omit dependencies, fragment sequential reasoning, or propagate errors; the independent causal contribution of fresh context is unmeasured.
- **Current hypothesis:** Fresh execution may reduce contamination only when external state is sufficiently complete to preserve dependencies required for correction and integration.
- **Unresolved question:** Whether persistent staged and fresh staged workflows can receive identical intermediate messages, evidence, artifacts, verifier inputs, samples, selectors, and final integration access.

No accepted branch evidence currently establishes the direction or magnitude of a fresh-boundary effect under such controls.

## Candidate Hypotheses and Possibilities

### C0002-EXP-01 — Boundary-only null

- **Status:** Hypothesis.
- **Claim:** When stage instructions, visible evidence, intermediate artifacts, candidate opportunities, verifier inputs, selector rules, and full charged compute are matched, changing only persistent versus fresh invocation boundaries produces no stable average difference in unsupported claims or cumulative reasoning errors.
- **Distinguishing information:** A matched contrast in which the only intended manipulation is access to prior conversational context beyond the standardized stage input.

### C0002-EXP-02 — Contamination-reduction benefit

- **Status:** Hypothesis.
- **Claim:** Fresh invocation boundaries reduce the preservation or amplification of earlier unsupported claims by preventing later stages from inheriting unstructured prior reasoning, even when standardized artifacts are shared.
- **Distinguishing information:** Error-lineage data showing fewer unsupported claims preserved or amplified in the fresh condition, without reduced factual coverage or task completion.

### C0002-EXP-03 — Interface-loss harm

- **Status:** Hypothesis.
- **Claim:** Fresh invocation boundaries increase omission, mutation, or misinterpretation of dependencies because standardized handoffs cannot fully encode the information available in a persistent context.
- **Distinguishing information:** Higher rates of dependency loss, contradiction, or error mutation in fresh conditions, especially where later stages need qualifications or relationships not explicitly represented in the handoff.

### C0002-EXP-04 — State-completeness threshold

- **Status:** Hypothesis.
- **Claim:** The effect of fresh invocation is non-monotonic or thresholded: fresh boundaries are harmful with incomplete state, neutral once essential dependencies are represented, and potentially beneficial when the state is sufficiently complete to preserve needed information while excluding distracting or unsupported reasoning.
- **Distinguishing information:** A factorial comparison across multiple predefined state-completeness levels rather than a single “state present versus absent” contrast.

### C0002-EXP-05 — Structured-state dominance

- **Status:** Hypothesis.
- **Claim:** Once a structured state artifact explicitly records claims, statuses, provenance, objections, unresolved questions, and stage outputs, the state representation explains most of the effect, and persistent versus fresh execution adds little.
- **Distinguishing information:** Similar outcomes across fresh and persistent conditions that share the same structured state, combined with larger differences between structured and weakly structured state conditions.

### C0002-EXP-06 — Hidden-context privilege

- **Status:** Hypothesis.
- **Claim:** A persistent workflow has an informational advantage whenever later stages can inspect prior conversational material not reproduced in the standardized handoff, even if that material is noisy. This makes the contrast unequal unless persistent agents are restricted to the same explicit stage package.
- **Distinguishing information:** Separate persistent conditions with unrestricted prior-context access and with prior context masked or replaced by the standardized package.

### C0002-EXP-07 — Compression-as-treatment

- **Status:** Hypothesis.
- **Claim:** The form and compression level of the handoff may have a larger effect than the invocation boundary. Concise handoffs may remove unsupported reasoning but also remove valid dependencies; exhaustive handoffs may preserve dependencies but reintroduce contamination and consume more compute.
- **Distinguishing information:** Matched fresh and persistent contrasts across concise, structured, and high-retention handoff formats, with handoff-generation and context-processing costs recorded.

### C0002-EXP-08 — Stage-specific sensitivity

- **Status:** Speculation.
- **Claim:** Invocation boundaries may matter more before challenge, evidence evaluation, or integration than before generative stages, because those stages depend differently on independence from earlier framing and on access to detailed dependencies.
- **Distinguishing information:** Boundary-placement comparisons that reset context at selected stage transitions rather than only comparing fully persistent with fully fresh workflows.
- **Speculative flag:** The branch has not established which stage transitions are most sensitive.

## Possible Mechanisms or Connections

1. **Selective information exclusion:** A fresh context may exclude unsupported reasoning that was never promoted into the structured artifact. This could reduce contamination, but only if the promotion rules do not also exclude valid qualifications or dependencies.

2. **Dependency preservation:** Persistent context may retain relationships among claims, objections, evidence, and caveats that a handoff does not encode. A structured state schema could recover some of this value without preserving all prior reasoning.

3. **Epistemic typing as a filter:** The protocol’s distinctions among sourced fact, interpretation, derived conclusion, hypothesis, speculation, objection, and unresolved question could make state transfer more precise. The relevant mechanism would be the typed representation, not freshness by itself.

4. **Handoff transformation:** Any summarization, normalization, or compression between stages can introduce omissions or mutate claim status. This transformation should be treated as an explicit factor rather than an invisible implementation detail.

5. **Context-access asymmetry:** A persistent condition may have both the standardized artifact and the full earlier trace, while a fresh condition has only the artifact. That contrast mixes invocation boundary with information quantity and retrieval opportunity.

6. **Cost-mediated equivalence:** A more complete handoff may improve informational equivalence but consume additional generation and context-processing budget. Under fixed compute, that cost may reduce the budget available for evidence analysis, verification, or integration.

7. **Coverage-error tradeoff:** A fresh condition might reduce unsupported claims simply by omitting more content. The accepted measurement requirement therefore connects directly to this frontier: unsupported-claim reduction must be interpreted alongside factual coverage, citation completeness, task completion, and usefulness.

## Questions for Internal Evidence Research

1. Which exact information objects are currently created by the B001 protocol at each stage, and which of them are available to each subsequent role?
2. Which protocol fields or output sections can serve as a canonical stage package for both persistent and fresh conditions?
3. Does the existing branch state distinguish raw stage output from accepted state, and would using accepted state during an active cycle introduce a stage-access asymmetry?
4. Which treatment features are inseparable from fresh execution in the current protocol, and which can be independently held constant in a branch-local experiment?
5. What minimum dependency types must a state schema preserve: claim identity, epistemic status, support reference, scope, uncertainty, objections, contradictions, open questions, and lineage links?
6. Where could hidden information enter the persistent condition through prior conversation history, role instructions, or unrecorded reasoning?
7. Can the same exact serialized stage package be supplied to both conditions while restricting the persistent condition from using earlier unstructured context?
8. What branch-local definitions could distinguish “state completeness,” “handoff completeness,” and “informational equivalence” without claiming they are identical?
9. Which existing accepted claims imply mandatory outcome controls, especially coverage, task completion, and stage-by-stage error lineage?
10. What cost categories must be charged when constructing, reading, compressing, or validating the stage package?

## Questions for External Evidence Research

1. Are there primary studies that compare persistent versus reset or fresh contexts while holding prompts, visible intermediate artifacts, evidence, sampling opportunities, and total inference cost approximately constant?
2. Are there controlled studies of context reset, context masking, state serialization, or handoff compression that measure factual error propagation or correction across stages?
3. What primary-source methods exist for measuring information loss or semantic mutation across sequential handoffs?
4. Are there benchmark designs that manipulate state completeness or memory fidelity independently of agent count and role labels?
5. Are there studies that compare access to full prior traces against access to structured summaries or claim ledgers under matched cost?
6. What evidence exists on whether structured claim status, provenance, objections, or uncertainty fields improve later verification or integration?
7. Are there established methods for tracing the lineage of a claim or error through multiple generation and revision stages?
8. Which studies explicitly charge prompt tokens, context processing, handoff generation, state construction, completion tokens, candidate generation, selection, and final-answer costs?
9. Are there primary studies showing that lower factual-error counts can be explained by reduced answer coverage or shorter outputs in context-reset or staged systems?
10. What evaluator designs have been used to distinguish omission, contradiction, unsupported preservation, correction, mutation, and amplification across stages?

## Potential Tests or Comparisons

### 1. Primary 2 × 2 boundary-by-state comparison

Compare:

- persistent context + weak stage package;
- fresh invocation + weak stage package;
- persistent context + structured complete-as-specified stage package;
- fresh invocation + structured complete-as-specified stage package.

The purpose is to estimate both a boundary effect and a boundary-by-state interaction. “Complete-as-specified” must mean compliance with a declared schema, not a claim that every relevant fact has actually been preserved.

### 2. Restricted-persistent comparison

Run a persistent workflow in which each stage receives only the same serialized stage package supplied to the fresh workflow, with prior unstructured trace hidden or unavailable. Compare this with unrestricted persistent access. This separates invocation continuity from hidden-context privilege.

### 3. Handoff-retention comparison

Hold the invocation boundary constant and vary the handoff representation:

- concise narrative summary;
- structured typed claim ledger;
- higher-retention package containing claim relations, objections, uncertainties, and lineage links.

Record both outcome changes and the compute cost of creating and consuming each package.

### 4. Boundary-placement comparison

Reset context only at selected transitions, such as before criticism, evidence assessment, or integration. This could identify whether any effect is concentrated at particular stage boundaries rather than attributable to “fresh roles” as a single undifferentiated treatment.

### 5. Dependency-injection probes

Construct tasks containing explicit dependencies, qualifications, contradictions, and unresolved questions. Track whether each item is preserved, omitted, mutated, corrected, or amplified across stage packages and final synthesis.

### 6. Coverage-matched evaluation

Compare unsupported-claim and error-lineage outcomes only alongside claim count, supported factual coverage, citation completeness, task completion, and response usefulness. Consider matched-output or minimum-coverage analyses so that omission is not misread as epistemic improvement.

### 7. Dual compute accounting

For every condition, record both the branch’s primary fixed-compute measure and a full secondary ledger covering prompt and context processing, completions, state creation, handoffs, tools, candidate generation, selection, and final integration. The exact primary rule remains unresolved and should not be assumed by the Explorer.

## Main Uncertainties

- “Informational equivalence” may be unattainable in a literal sense because persistent access and fresh access differ by definition. The operational target may need to be equality of authorized visible inputs rather than equality of all internal informational effects.
- A state schema can be complete relative to declared fields while still omitting task-specific dependencies.
- Restricting a persistent workflow to a serialized package may turn it into a functional reset even if the underlying invocation remains persistent.
- More complete state can consume more compute, creating a tradeoff between information preservation and reasoning budget.
- The same handoff can be interpreted differently across invocations, so byte-identical inputs do not guarantee functionally identical use.
- Boundary effects may depend on task structure, stage location, verifier quality, and evidence access; a single average effect could conceal positive and negative regimes.
- The branch does not yet have an accepted state-completeness metric, error-lineage coding protocol, evaluator reliability threshold, or primary compute-matching rule.
- C0002-EXP-08 is explicitly speculative; no accepted branch evidence identifies the most sensitive stage boundary.
